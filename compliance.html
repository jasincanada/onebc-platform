Roadmap · the rules

How a call stays inside the rules.

This is political canvassing. The people on the line are voters and candidates. The ask for support is made by a person in the room, not by a recording.

The phone rules below are the federal ones published by the CRTC. They apply to these calls whether the election is provincial or federal. A provincial election can add its own advertising rules. This page is the design. It is not a letter from a lawyer, and no regulator has certified the desk. The software is still a draft. No live call has been placed.

Checked against the CRTC pages on 29 September 2026: rules for political entities and calls from parties and candidates.

What the recording is allowed to do

A political party or a candidate may not use a recording to ask for a vote, money, or support, unless that person already agreed to receive that specific recording. The desk refuses that script. The recording says who is calling, why, and how to reach a person. It asks yes or no. Then it stops.

The CRTC also treats a recording that holds someone until a caller is free as the kind of call that needs prior agreement. The desk does not do that. If no one in the room is free, the call ends. A person calls back later. The recording does not dial that number again.

What every call must say at the start

The opening has to be clear, and it has to come first.

  • The name of the party, the riding, or the candidate on whose behalf the call is made.
  • The purpose, in one short line.
  • A local or toll-free number, and an email address or a mailing address, where a person can be reached. Those must still work for 60 days after the call.

The desk will not dial if any of those are missing. You can hear the opening on your own computer before the room uses it. That preview does not place a call.

Who may be called, and who must be left alone

Parties and candidates are exempt from the National Do Not Call List. They are not exempt from keeping their own do-not-call list. If someone asks to stop, they are removed, they are not transferred, and they are not called again.

The desk calls only a list you already have a right to call. You confirm that in one sentence before anything dials. It does not invent numbers, and it does not walk every number in an exchange. A number is called once. Emergency lines are not on a voter list and are not generated by the desk.

The hours

On a weekday the legal window is 9:00 a.m. to 9:30 p.m. On a weekend it is 10:00 a.m. to 6:00 p.m. Those are local hours. The room works in the daytime, Pacific time, inside that window. Outside the hours you set, and outside the legal window, nothing dials.

Privacy

The list belongs to the party. It is used for this riding. It is not copied into the software package. The callers in the room cannot download the whole list. The logs do not keep a full phone number.

Recording is off unless you turn it on. Turning it on asks you to confirm again. If it is on, the opening says so. A person who asks to be removed is removed whether or not the call was recorded.

If a federal election is underway

During a federal election, some callers must register with the CRTC Voter Contact Registry within 48 hours of the first call. That includes anyone using a recording to contact voters, and anyone who hires a calling service. A registered party making only live calls from its own people is not the same case. If a recording is used, or a phone company is placing the calls, registration has to be checked before the first call. The desk does not file that registration for you.

If this is a British Columbia election

Elections BC treats some automated calls, and some paid canvassing, as election advertising. That can require an authorization line: who authorized it, and a B.C. phone number, mailing address, or email. Live calls from your own people, not done as a paid advertising service, are treated differently. Confirm with Elections BC which side this shift is on before the first day. Their public note is Know the rules. The date on that note is the 2024 election. The rule to check is the one in force for this campaign.

What “handled” means here

A call does not start unless the list is permitted, the opening is complete, the hours are legal, and outbound has been turned on by you in a separate step. A person who asks to stop is finished. A recording never makes the ask. Nobody is left on hold waiting for a free caller.

That is the control. It is not a certificate. Until the desk is finished and you have confirmed the election rules that apply to this riding, outbound stays off.
